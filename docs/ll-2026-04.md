# LL-2026-04, and what Custody actually claims

Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-04, *Governance framework on use of artificial
intelligence and machine learning*, was issued 8 April 2026 and took effect
**6 August 2026** (120 days later). It applies to seller/servicers using AI/ML in
connection with originating loans sold to or guaranteed by Fannie Mae, or
servicing loans on Fannie Mae's behalf.

Primary source:
<https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/news-events/lender-letter-ll-2026-04-governance-framework-use-artificial-intelligence-and-machine-learning>

Read the letter. Do not rely on this document, and in particular do not rely on
vendor summaries of it — including the ones that circulated a specific
eight-field audit-record schema the letter does not contain.

---

## What the letter requires

Paraphrased from the letter and from the analyses by
[Cooley](https://finsights.cooley.com/fannie-mae-issues-ai-ml-governance-framework-for-sellers-and-servicers/)
and [Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-04-16/fannie-mae-issues-ai-and-machine-learning-governance-framework/):

1. **Policies and procedures** covering the development, implementation, use and
   maintenance of any AI/ML system, and the measuring and managing of AI/ML risk.
   At minimum those policies must be transparent and communicated to appropriate
   personnel; incorporate the characteristics of trustworthy and ethical AI/ML;
   reflect an understanding of legal and regulatory requirements; align with the
   seller/servicer's risk tolerance; and be **reviewed at least annually**.
2. **Compliance with Fannie Mae's Information Security and Business Resiliency
   Supplement.**
3. **Vendor and subcontractor governance** that is *"no less protective"* than
   the framework's own requirements.
4. **Disclosure on request.** Upon Fannie Mae's request, promptly disclose the
   types of AI/ML used, the purpose and manner of that use, the safeguards
   implemented to mitigate related risks, and such other information as Fannie
   Mae may require.

## What the letter does not require

It does **not** specify a per-decision record schema. It does not name the fields
`principal, model, endpoint, redacted prompt, response treatment, policy version,
decision outcome, timestamp`. It does not require append-only storage,
cryptographic signing, or a log queryable by loan, date, model and principal.

Those are one implementation of obligations 1 and 4 — a reasonable one, and the
one this library takes — but they are Custody's design choices, not Fannie Mae's
instructions. Any vendor telling you the letter mandates them is selling you
something.

## What Custody actually claims

**A lender can be asked, at any time, to promptly describe the purpose and manner
of its AI use and the safeguards it has in place.** There are two ways to answer.

One is a document describing what you intend to happen. The other is a record of
what did happen, per decision, that the person asking can verify without taking
your word for it.

Custody produces the second. That is the whole claim.

| Letter obligation | How Custody helps | Honest limit |
|---|---|---|
| Policies covering *use* of AI/ML | `Ledger(policy=...)` stamps a policy version on every record, so what was in force when a decision was made is evidenced rather than remembered | Custody does not write your policy or review it annually |
| **Safeguards** implemented to mitigate risk | `verify.gate` is a live, deterministic safeguard: figures must appear in a supplied source document, fields must cite one, classifications must land in a closed set, and anything below the confidence floor routes to a person. Each verdict is recorded with its reason | These are *our* four checks. Whether they are the right safeguards for your risk tolerance is your judgement |
| **Purpose and manner** of use, disclosed promptly | `examiner.packet(ledger, loan)` returns every AI decision on a loan, in order, with model, purpose, verdict, the human who reviewed it, and what was written | Only for calls actually routed through Custody |
| Evidence that survives scrutiny | Hash chaining plus Ed25519 signing make alteration and deletion detectable by the recipient, without access to your systems | Tamper-*evidence*, not tamper-proofing. Anyone with write access can destroy the file; they cannot quietly change it |
| Annual policy review | Every record carries its policy version, so "what changed, and which decisions ran under the old policy" is a query | The review itself is organisational |

## What Custody does not address at all

- **AI/ML inventory.** Custody sees the models it is asked to wrap. An inventory
  built only from what happened to be instrumented is worse than none, because it
  looks complete.
- **Governance programme and executive ownership.** Organisational.
- **Information Security and Business Resiliency Supplement compliance.** Out of
  scope entirely.
- **Vendor and subcontractor governance.** Contractual.
- **Bias and fair-lending testing.** A real and separate obligation under ECOA and
  fair-lending law, with mature vendors already serving it. Custody makes no
  disparate-impact claim of any kind.

## Two design decisions worth arguing with

**Committing over a rejection is permitted, and recorded as an override.** A gate
that makes an override impossible gets bypassed entirely, and then nothing is
recorded at all. `disposition` distinguishes `committed` from
`committed_over_rejection`, so an override is visible rather than absent.

**Rejected decisions stay in the packet.** Filtering them out would produce a
cleaner artifact and a false one. An audit trail showing only the successes is a
brochure.
